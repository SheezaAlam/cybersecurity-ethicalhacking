# IT SECURITY POLICIES & PROCEDURES

## Introduction to Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC)

### **a. Governance**

* A framework of **rules, roles, and processes** that ensure IT supports business goals.
* Examples: **COBIT, COBOT, ITIL**.

### **b. Risk Management**

* Identifying, assessing, and mitigating risks that may harm the organization.
* Example: Creating criticality levels for each risk and setting countermeasures based on impact.

### **c. Compliance**

* Ensuring the organization meets internal policies and external regulations (GDPR, HIPAA, ISO 27001).

> **Note:** Security policies and procedures form the *Governance Backbone* — they define expectations, responsibilities, and controls to manage risk and maintain compliance.

---

## Why Policies and Procedures Matter

* Translate governance goals into action.
* Standardize behavior across employees.
* Support regulatory compliance & audits.
* Reduce security incidents by preventing risky behavior.
* **Example:** Strong password policy + implementation procedure prevents weak credentials.

---

## Understanding IT Security Policies

### **What are Security Policies?**

Security policies are **formal, high-level documents** that:

* Define what must be protected and why.
* Describe acceptable & unacceptable behavior.
* Reflect management's commitment.

They answer: **WHAT** and **WHY**.

**Example:** "All laptops must use full-disk encryption to protect data at rest."

### **Objectives of Security Policies**

* Protect information assets.
* Ensure business continuity.
* Promote consistent decision-making.
* Support audits & compliance.

---

## Characteristics of Good Security Policies

1. **Clear & Concise** – easy for all employees to understand.
2. **Enforceable** – technically & practically possible.
3. **Aligned with Business Goals** – should not reduce productivity.
4. **Management Approved** – ensures authority.
5. **Regularly Reviewed** – updated for new risks.

---

## Common Types of IT Security Policies

| Policy Type                      | Description                                | Example                                    |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)**  | Rules for using company IT resources       | No unauthorized software downloads         |
| **Password Policy**              | Defines complexity, length, expiration     | 12+ characters with symbols                |
| **Data Classification Policy**   | Defines data sensitivity levels            | Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted |
| **Access Control Policy**        | Decides who accesses what                  | Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)           |
| **Incident Response Policy**     | Steps for handling a breach                | Report within 1 hour                       |
| **Network Security Policy**      | Secure configuration of firewalls, IDS/IPS | No unapproved open ports                   |
| **Email & Communication Policy** | Rules for company communication tools      | No forwarding data to personal email       |

---

## Policy Lifecycle

1. **Drafting** – by security/compliance teams.
2. **Review & Approval** – management/legal review.
3. **Distribution** – shared with employees.
4. **Implementation** – via training & tools.
5. **Monitoring & Auditing** – check compliance.
6. **Review & Update** – keep relevant.

# Understanding Procedures

## What are Security Procedures?

* **Step-by-step instructions** that explain how to implement a policy.
* They answer: **HOW**.

### Example:

**Policy:** "All servers must be hardened before deployment."

**Procedure:**

1. Disable unused ports.
2. Remove default credentials.
3. Install security patches.

---

## Purposes of Procedures

* Ensure consistent execution.
* Reduce human error.
* Enable auditing & accountability.
* Help new staff perform tasks correctly.

---

## Characteristics of Effective Procedures

* Detailed but clear.
* Written in simple language.
* Includes responsible roles.
* References related policies.

---

## Linking Policy & Procedure

| Policy                                | Procedure                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Employees must use complex passwords. | 1. Set length to 12. 2. Include numbers/symbols. 3. Change every 90 days. |
| Systems must be patched regularly.    | 1. Weekly scan. 2. Test patches. 3. Deploy in maintenance window.         |

---

## Relationship Between Policies, Standards & Procedures

### **Hierarchy**

1. **Policies** – high-level intentions.
2. **Standards** – specific required rules.
3. **Procedures** – step-by-step guidance.

### Example (Password Management)

* **Policy:** Users must maintain secure passwords.
* **Standard:** Minimum 12 characters.
* **Procedure:** Follow these steps to change password.

---

## Importance of IT Security Policies & Procedures

* Legal & organizational foundation.
* Defines roles & responsibilities.
* Supports compliance (GDPR, PCI-DSS, HIPAA).
* Helps with incident management.
* Promotes awareness.
* Without them, security becomes reactive.

---

## Challenges in Policy Implementation

* Lack of management support.
* Poor employee awareness.
* Policies too strict or complicated.
* Outdated policies.

---

## Best Practices for Policy Development

1. Involve all departments.
2. Align with legal & business needs.
3. Keep policies simple and readable.
4. Train employees regularly.
5. Audit yearly.
6. Store in a central repository.

---

## Summary

* **Policies = What & Why**
* **Procedures = How**
* Together, they form the foundation of **IT Governance**.
* Improve consistency, accountability, and compliance.
