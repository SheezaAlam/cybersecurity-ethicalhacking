# **SOCIAL ENGINEERING ATTACKS & COUNTERMEASURES**

---

## **Introduction**

### **What is Social Engineering?**

**Definition:**
Social Engineering is the psychological manipulation of people to make them reveal confidential information, perform certain actions, or bypass normal security procedures.

**Key Point:**
Unlike technical hacking (which targets machines), social engineering **targets human psychology**.

### **Why It Works**

Humans are often the weakest link in security. Attackers exploit:

* Trust in authority
* Emotional reactions (fear, urgency)
* Curiosity
* Carelessness or lack of awareness

---

## **Psychology Behind Social Engineering**

Attackers rely on human emotions and predictable behavior.

### **Common Psychological Triggers**

* **Authority:** "I’m calling from HR/IT—give me your details."
* **Urgency:** "Your account will be deleted in 10 minutes!"
* **Greed:** "You won a prize—click here to claim."
* **Fear:** "Your computer is infected—install this software now."
* **Curiosity:** Mysterious links, files, or USB drives.

### **Scenario Example**

Attacker calls pretending to be IT Support:
“Your account shows unusual activity. I need your login details to secure it.”
Victim gives details due to **authority + urgency**.

---

## **Common Types of Social Engineering Attacks**

### **A. Phishing**

Deceptive messages/emails/websites trick users into revealing sensitive info.

**Variants:**

* **Spear Phishing:** Targeted at specific person/company.
* **Whaling:** Targeting CEOs or executives.
* **Vishing:** Phone call phishing.
* **Smishing:** SMS phishing.

**Example:**
Email from "rnicrosoft-support" asking to verify account.

---

### **B. Baiting**

Using tempting items to lure victims.

**Example:**
USB labeled *“Employee Salaries 2025”* left in parking lot → malware infection.

---

### **C. Pretexting**

Creating a believable fake story to extract information.

**Example:**
Pretending to be a system admin asking for login details.

---

### **D. Tailgating**

Following someone into a restricted area without authorization.

**Example:**
Attacker carrying coffee pretends card isn’t working.

---

### **E. Quid Pro Quo**

Exchange something for information.

**Example:**
“Give me your login details and I’ll give you money.”

---

### **F. Dumpster Diving**

Searching trash for confidential information.

**Example:**
Finding printed passwords or financial data in garbage.

---

### **G. Shoulder Surfing**

Stealing information by watching someone’s screen or keyboard.

**Example:**
Seeing someone enter a PIN at ATM.

---

### **H. Impersonation**

Pretending to be a legitimate person like IT staff.

**Example:**
Attacker with fake ID badge enters office claiming to be technician.

---

# **Countermeasures & Prevention**

## **A. User Awareness & Training**

* Regular security training
* Verify sender identity before clicking links
* Never share passwords over phone/email
* Report suspicious incidents quickly

---

## **B. Technical Defenses**

* Email filters
* Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
* Endpoint protection
* Proper access controls

---

## **C. Organizational Policies**

* Clean desk policy
* Shred confidential documents
* Security rules for visitors/contractors
* Conduct simulated phishing tests

---

## **D. Physical Security Measures**

* Security guards + access cards
* Enforce “No tailgating” rule
* CCTV and visitor badges

---

## **E. Incident Response**

Employees should know:

* Who to contact
* How to report phishing or impersonation attempts
* Follow a clear reporting chain

---

# **Real-World Social Engineering Cases**

### **1. RSA Breach (2011)**

* Phishing email titled *“2011 Recruitment Plan”*
* Malware installed → SecurID system compromised

### **2. Twitter Bitcoin Scam (2020)**

* Social engineering internal employees
* High-profile accounts hijacked

### **3. Target Breach (2013)**

* Attackers phished an HVAC contractor
* Gained access to Target network → 40M card details stolen

---

# **Summary**

* Social Engineering = **Hacking Humans**
* Exploits trust, fear, urgency, curiosity
* Attacks include: Phishing, Baiting, Pretexting, Tailgating, etc.
* Best defense = Awareness + Verification + Multi-layer security
